# KG-0009: Historical derivation of the 5 m `flddif` threshold

- Gap ID: `KG-0009`
- Linked event IDs: `EV-0170`, `EV-0174`, `EV-0175`
- Category: scientific threshold / historical rationale
- Evidence basis: legacy source plus maintainer-confirmed scientific purpose; numerical selection basis remains unknown
- Status: partially_formalized

## What remains unknown?

The maintainer clarified the scientific purpose of the elevation filter: `prmwat` should include permanent water only where CaMa-Flood could reproduce inundation. Isolated permanent lakes high on hills lie outside the model's expected flood extent and must not be subtracted as existing simulated floodwater. The historical numerical basis for choosing 5 m rather than another cutoff (calibration, empirical selection, or inheritance) remains unknown.

## How was it discovered?

Review of the comparison in `map/src/src_param/calc_prmwat.F90` and the migration task's warning not to invent a derivation first identified the uncertainty. The maintainer later supplied the physical role of the filter. No immediate output change is needed to document that rationale.

## Verified facts

- The legacy code accepts `flddif <= 5` for pixels in the selected `rivwth` classes.
- The approved migration requires preserving that rule.
- The maintainer says the filter excludes permanent water outside CaMa-Flood's reproducible inundation domain, including isolated high-elevation lakes.
- The maintainer described the intended criterion as `flddif < 5 m`, while current v4 and v5 code use `<= 5 m`; the exact-equality behavior is recorded separately in KG-0011.
- Mekong regression found no selected pixels with `flddif=-9999`; this does not determine the threshold's scientific basis.

## How was the knowledge formalized?

The v5 source, parameter README, map metadata specification, and `prmwat` registry row now explain the model-representable inundation rationale. ADR-0004 preserves the existing comparator pending a separate decision on the exact-5 m boundary. The historical numerical derivation of the value 5 remains undocumented.

## Resolution trace

- Resolution method: mixed (source review and maintainer knowledge)
- Evidence or maintainer explanation: maintainer clarification in DR-0033 explains the physical scope of the filter; the exact numerical derivation remains unverified.
- Final interpretation and rationale: keep permanent-water reference area within the flood extent CaMa-Flood can reproduce, excluding isolated high-elevation lakes. Preserve the current v4/v5 5 m comparison until its equality boundary is confirmed; do not claim that 5 m was calibrated.
- Formalization locations: v5 source, parameter READMEs, map metadata specification, `prmwat` registry row, ADR-0004
- Human review: pending

## Verification and limits

This rationale is sufficient to document why an elevation filter exists. Its transferability and the quantitative choice of 5 m need historical method records or scientific evaluation before changing the threshold.

## Why the gap matters

Changing or reusing the threshold in future products without understanding its numerical basis could affect area estimates.
