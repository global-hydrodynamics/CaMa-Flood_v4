# KG-0012: Scientific rationale for legacy allocation thresholds

- Gap ID: KG-0012
- Linked event IDs: EV-0179
- Category: scientific assumption / numerical thresholds
- Evidence basis: source-code inspection; historical rationale not found in inspected repository materials
- Status: open

## What remains unknown?

What scientific or empirical rationale established the thresholds and ranking weights used by the legacy flow-gauge, dam, and level-gauge allocation programs?

## How was it discovered?

The Phase A migration inventory traced the matching and classification branches directly. The code contains fixed search windows, area cutoffs, score penalties, upstream candidate limits, and fallback rules that affect selected cells and output classes. No rationale for these numerical values was found in source comments or inspected workflow documentation. The question was recorded during the task rather than reconstructed from a private historical discussion.

## Verified facts

- All three programs search a 7 by 7 high-resolution pixel window and require candidate upstream area above 5 percent of input area.
- The candidate score adjusts relative area error by 0.02 per pixel and caps the underestimation score at 1000.
- All build at most eight immediate upstream coarse-cell candidates and reject tested alternatives below 10 percent of input area.
- Flow gauges consider a downstream mismatch above 5 percent, allow one or two upstream substitutions according to single/multi, stop below 10 percent error, and mark a no-substitute result unmatched when the selected area exceeds the input area by more than 100 percent.
- Dam allocation considers upstream substitutions above 20 percent error, classifies catchment area below 30 percent of ctmare as small/sub-grid, and also uses a greater-than-100-percent error fallback.
- Exact source locations and distinctions are tabulated in docs/map_allocation_migration_plan.md.

## How was the knowledge formalized?

The Phase A plan lists each observed rule and requires parity characterization before any proposed numerical change. No threshold has been changed or endorsed by this record.

- Final interpretation and rationale: preserve as frozen legacy behavior during mechanical migration unless the maintainer explicitly approves a scientific change.

## Verification and limits

Source inspection verifies the current expressions and branch behavior. It does not establish why values were selected, their empirical calibration, or whether they remain scientifically appropriate.

## Why the gap matters

Changing any threshold or tie rule can alter station/dam placement or output classification. A migration needs a reproducible compatibility baseline, and future scientific revision needs the original rationale or an explicit new validation basis.
